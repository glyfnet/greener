# Habitat on a balcony, courtyard or rented site

A small space can still feed and shelter wildlife. Greener plans for apartments, balconies, courtyards and rented homes keep every action removable unless the owner or strata agrees in writing.

## What works in a small space

- Plant in pots, troughs and hanging baskets. Use lightweight pots if you will move them, and good potting mix. Plastic pots hold water longer than terracotta or concrete. [kb:nsw-sydney-nature-small-spaces]
- Match plants to the light. In Sydney a north-facing balcony gets the most winter sun, an east-facing one gentle morning sun, and a west-facing one harsh afternoon sun. Windy, exposed balconies need hardy plants. [kb:nsw-sydney-nature-small-spaces]
- Add water. A shallow birdbath helps birds and bees. Keep it clean and topped up. [kb:nsw-sydney-nature-small-spaces]
- Add shelter for insects, such as a bee or insect hotel. [kb:nsw-sydney-nature-small-spaces]
- Plant in layers: a small shrub, a climber on a trellis or mesh, and groundcovers or grasses, so different animals find food and cover. [kb:city-of-sydney-urban-habitat-guide]
- Do not use poisons. They harm people and the insects that small birds eat. [kb:nsw-sydney-nature-small-spaces]
- Choose local natives. Greener checks every plant against the species recorded near your site, and blocks declared weeds. [kb:greener-how-plans-work]

## Before you start

- Check your lease or strata by-laws. Ask before you fix anything to walls, floors or railings, and before you add heavy planters.
- Check the weight your balcony can carry. Wet soil is heavy. If you are not sure, ask the building manager or strata, and use fewer, lighter pots.
- Make sure water drains to the balcony drain and does not run onto neighbours below.
- Keep pots and baskets secure, so wind cannot blow them off the balcony.

## Going further

- Ask your council about habitat programs, free plants or workshops for residents. [kb:city-of-sydney-urban-habitat-guide]
- Talk to your strata about habitat in shared gardens and courtyards. [kb:city-of-sydney-urban-habitat-guide]
